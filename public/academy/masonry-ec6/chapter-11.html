11.1 Purpose of this chapter

This chapter explains how wind pressure from EN 1991-1-4 becomes the design lateral pressure used in masonry panel design. It is an interface chapter: it connects the wind action calculation to the Eurocode 6 flexural checks used in Chapters 9 and 10.

Scope note. This chapter does not replace a full wind-loading calculation. The correct wind zones, coefficients and National Annex values must be selected before this interface step is used.

11.2 Key idea

A masonry wall panel is not designed using the basic wind speed directly. The design starts from wind pressure on the building surface. This pressure is then factored as a design action and used as the lateral load in the masonry bending check.

1 · Wind pressureDetermine qp(z) from the wind calculation.
2 · CoefficientsSelect cpe and cpi for the wall condition.
3 · Net pressureCalculate Wk from external and internal pressure.
4 · ULS factorApply γQ to obtain WEd.
5 · EC6 inputUse WEd in the panel moment check.
6 · Panel checkCheck MEd ≤ MRd.

Clean interface sketch: wind pressure is converted into WEd before EC6 panel design.

11.3 Wind pressure notation

For a wall panel, the characteristic net pressure can be written in a simplified teaching form as:

Wk = qp(z) \,[cpe − cpi]

The design lateral pressure is then obtained by applying the wind action partial factor:

WEd = γQ Wk

For the common case where wind is the leading variable action at ULS, γQ = 1.50 is commonly used with the relevant National Annex.

External pressure

we = qp(z)cpe

Internal pressure

wi = qp(z)cpi

Net pressure

Wk = we − wi

11.4 Practical notes for masonry design

Coefficient selection

  • Use the pressure coefficient matching the actual wall zone and wind direction.
  • Check pressure and suction where both can govern.
  • Confirm the internal pressure condition from building openings.

Panel design input

  • Use a consistent sign convention.
  • For cavity walls, Chapter 12 explains load sharing between leaves.
  • If the wall has large openings, the panel size and support condition may change.
Design link. The output from this chapter, WEd, becomes the input pressure in the EC6 panel formula such as MEd = αWEdl².

Calculation sheet

ReferenceCalculationOutput

Wk = we − wi = 0.52 − (−0.195)		Wk = 0.715 kN/m²

WEd = γQWk = 1.50 × 0.715		WEd = 1.07 kN/m²

Result. The design lateral pressure to be used in the masonry panel design is WEd = 1.07 kN/m².
Verification note. The pressure coefficients used in this worked example are teaching values. For a real project, cpe and cpi must be selected from the current EN 1991-1-4 tables and the relevant National Annex for the actual building geometry, wind direction, wall zone and opening condition.

11.6 Chapter summary

  • Chapter 11 converts wind pressure from EN 1991-1-4 into the design pressure used by EC6 panel design.
  • Wk is the characteristic net pressure on the wall.
  • WEd is the factored design pressure used in MEd = αWEdl².
  • The most important practical decision is selecting the correct pressure coefficients for the wall zone and building condition.

Common mistakes to flag

Using basic wind speed directly in the masonry panel calculation.
Mixing pressure and suction signs without a clear sign convention.
Using a centre-zone coefficient for an edge or corner wall zone.
Forgetting that openings can change internal pressure and panel geometry.

Source notes used for Chapter 11

EN 1991-1-4 wind actions, especially surface pressure notation in §5.2 and pressure coefficients in §7.2; EN 1990 Eq. 6.10 for ULS load combinations; EN 1996-1-1 §6.3.1 and Annex E for the masonry panel design interface; Roberts and Brooker lateral resistance workflow.
