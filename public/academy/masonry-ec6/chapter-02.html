3. Masonry Units and Unit Strength

A masonry unit may be a clay brick, concrete block, calcium silicate unit, AAC block or natural stone unit. For design, the test strength of the unit is converted to a normalised mean compressive strength, fb, so that units of different sizes can be compared consistently. EN 772-1 / normalised strength

Use declared product data

Take unit type, declared compressive strength and unit group from the manufacturer or project specification.

Use fb in design

The normalised strength is the unit strength input used in the masonry strength equation.

Do not use brick strength directly

Masonry strength also depends on mortar, unit group, workmanship and National Annex values.

Key idea: a brick or block has its own strength, but the completed wall has a different strength because the unit, mortar and bond interact.

4. Unit Grouping

Eurocode 6 groups masonry units according to the volume and direction of holes, together with web and shell thickness. The group affects the coefficient K used in the fk equation. EN 1996-1-1 Table 3.1

5. Mortar Strength

Mortar contributes to bedding, durability and load transfer between units. A mortar class such as M4 means a mortar compressive strength of about 4 N/mm² at 28 days. EN 1996-1-1 §3.2 / EN 998-2

General-purpose mortar

Common for ordinary brick and block masonry and used in most teaching examples in this book.

G mortarordinary joints

Thin-layer mortar

Used with accurate units such as AAC blocks. The equation exponents and K values may change.

T mortarthin joints

Lightweight mortar

Used where low density and thermal performance are important. Use the density band required by the National Annex table.

L mortardensity based

6. Characteristic Compressive Strength of Masonry

For masonry with general-purpose mortar, the characteristic compressive strength is obtained from the unit strength, mortar strength and coefficient K. EN 1996-1-1 §3.6.1 UK NA Table NA.4

$$ fk = K\, fb^{\alpha}\, fm^{\beta} $$
Characteristic compressive strength equation. Use proper subscript notation: fk, fb and fm.
  • K depends on masonry unit material, unit group and mortar type.
  • α and β depend on the mortar system.
  • For general-purpose mortar, the common teaching form uses α = 0.7 and β = 0.3.
Reference table kept in pop-up: The full K coefficient table is available from the UK NA Table NA.4 reference chip above. The main page keeps only the design route and equation, while the detailed reference table opens in the pop-up window.

7. Small Cross-Section Factor

Small loaded cross-sections are more sensitive to local variation in units and workmanship. Therefore, where the loaded gross cross-sectional area is less than 0.1 m², the characteristic compressive strength is reduced. EN 1996-1-1 §3.6.1.2

$$ A < 0.1\;m^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad f_{k,red}=fk\,(0.7+3A) $$
A is the loaded gross cross-sectional area in m².
This check is easy to miss for narrow piers, returns and small masonry columns. The calculator below applies the factor automatically.

8. Design Compressive Strength

The design compressive strength is the characteristic strength divided by the material partial factor. The value of γM depends on the category of manufacturing control and the class of execution control. EN 1996-1-1 §2.4.3 UK NA Table NA.1

$$ f_d = \frac{fk}{\gamma_M} $$

For Sri Lankan teaching examples, Category II units and Class 2 execution are often assumed unless better quality control is confirmed. This gives a conservative route for early calculations.

Reference factors kept in pop-up: The γM tables are available from the EN 1996-1-1 §2.4.3 and UK NA Table NA.1 reference chips above. This keeps the theory page clean while still giving the complete reference when needed.

11. Common Mistakes to Flag

!

Using unit strength directly as masonry strength.

!

Selecting K without confirming unit group and mortar type.

!

Forgetting that α and β change for thin-layer mortar.

!

Forgetting the small cross-section factor for piers and columns.

!

Using γM from the wrong execution or control category.

!

Mixing N/mm² and kN/m² without conversion.
