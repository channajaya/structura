Chapter Position and Purpose

What this chapter prepares you to do

This chapter explains the theory for unreinforced masonry walls subjected mainly to vertical loading. Before a wall can be checked, the designer must know the effective height, effective thickness, slenderness ratio, eccentricity and capacity reduction factor.

1. Basic Vertical Resistance Check

Clause anchor: EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.2.1

At ultimate limit state, the design vertical load applied to the wall must not exceed the design vertical resistance:

$$N_{Ed} \leq N_{Rd}$$

This check is normally required at the top, middle and bottom of the wall, because the vertical load and eccentricity can vary over the height.

2. Effective Height

Clause anchor: EN 1996-1-1 §5.5.1.2

The effective height is obtained by applying a restraint factor to the clear height:

$$h_{ef}=\rho_n h$$

A wall with better restraint has a smaller effective height and better buckling resistance. Side stiffening walls can also reduce the buckling length if the stiffness and connection conditions are satisfied.

Clean teaching sketch — effective height depends on vertical and side restraint

3. Effective Thickness

Clause anchor: EN 1996-1-1 §5.5.1.3

For a single-leaf wall, the effective thickness is normally the actual wall thickness:

$$t_{ef}=t$$

For cavity walls, double-leaf walls, or walls stiffened by piers, the effective thickness may need a code-based calculation. For a wall stiffened by bonded piers, the simplified teaching form is:

$$t_{ef}=\rho_t t$$

The coefficient ρt depends on pier spacing, pier width, and the ratio of pier thickness to wall thickness.

4. Slenderness Ratio

Clause anchor: EN 1996-1-1 §5.5.2.1

The slenderness ratio measures the buckling sensitivity of the wall:

$$\frac{h_{ef}}{t_{ef}}$$

For unreinforced masonry walls subjected mainly to vertical load, the simplified design route is normally limited by:

$$\frac{h_{ef}}{t_{ef}} \leq 27$$
A wall may have enough material strength but still be unsuitable if it is too slender. Always check slenderness before relying on the vertical resistance equation.

5. Eccentricity

Clause anchor: EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.2.2 and EN 1996-1-1 §5.5.1.1

Eccentricity is the distance between the line of action of the vertical load and the wall centreline. It may come from floor bearing, applied moments, construction tolerances or lateral load.

$$e_i = \frac{M_{id}}{N_{id}} + e_{he} \pm e_{init}$$
$$e_{init}=\frac{h_{ef}}{450}$$
$$e_i \geq 0.05t$$

A minimum eccentricity is usually considered because no real wall is perfectly straight or perfectly loaded.

6. Capacity Reduction Factor

Clause anchor: EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.2.2 and Annex G

The capacity reduction factor Φ reduces the axial resistance to allow for eccentricity and slenderness. At the top or bottom of the wall:

$$\Phi_i = 1-2\frac{e_i}{t}$$

At mid-height, Annex G may be used. A common route is:

$$\Phi_m=A_1e^{-u^2/2}$$
$$A_1=1-2\frac{e_{mk}}{t}$$
$$\lambda=\frac{h_{ef}}{t_{ef}}\sqrt{\frac{fk}{E}}$$
$$u=\frac{\lambda-0.063}{0.73-1.17\frac{e_{mk}}{t}}$$

The governing reduction factor is normally the smaller value from the top/bottom and mid-height checks.

7. Design Vertical Resistance

Clause anchor: EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.2

For a wall strip, the design resistance is calculated from the reduced area capacity:

$$N_{Rd}=\Phi A f_d$$

For a single-leaf wall checked per metre run, the gross area is:

$$A=t\times1000\ \text{mm}^2\quad \text{per metre length}$$
$$N_{Rd}=\frac{\Phi\,t\,(1000)\,f_d}{1000}\quad \text{kN/m}$$

The final check remains:

$$N_{Ed}\leq N_{Rd}$$

9. Common Mistakes to Flag

Checking only one levelTop, middle and bottom can govern differently.
Using actual heightVertical resistance needs effective height, not only clear height.
Ignoring slab bearing eccentricityFloor reactions can create moments and reduce capacity.
Dropping ΦNever use only t × fd; the reduction factor is part of the resistance.
Using total cavity thicknessUse the thickness of the leaf carrying vertical load unless composite action is justified.
Forgetting the National AnnexMaterial factors and some design choices depend on the adopted National Annex.

Manual checking reference

Use EN 1996-1-1 §5.5.1.2 for effective height, §5.5.1.3 for effective thickness, §5.5.2.1 for slenderness, §6.1.2 for vertical resistance, and Annex G for the mid-height reduction factor. EN 1996-1-1 §6.1 provides the framework for vertical-load design.
