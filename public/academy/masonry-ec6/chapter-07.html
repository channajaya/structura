Chapter Position and Purpose

A concentrated load transfers force into masonry through a limited bearing area. The wall may be globally safe, but the small zone below the beam, lintel or padstone can still be overstressed.

Typical reader task: check whether a beam end reaction can safely bear on a masonry wall, or whether a padstone/spreader is required.

7.1 What Is a Concentrated Load?

A concentrated load is a vertical action transferred into masonry through a limited bearing area instead of a continuous line load along the full wall length. Common examples are beam end reactions, lintel reactions, padstone reactions, local column reactions and temporary support loads.

7.2 Physical Behaviour Under Bearing

A small bearing area creates high local compression. Masonry can carry more than a simple bearing-stress calculation may suggest because the load spreads down into the wall and nearby masonry provides confinement. This benefit is limited by edge distance, eccentricity and the available wall length.

Actual bearing area

The direct contact area under the beam, plate or padstone.

\[A_b=l_c w_c\]

Effective area

The area that can participate after load spread, limited by openings and edges.

\[A_{ef}=l_{ef}t\]

7.3 Basic Design Route

01Calculate \(N_{Edc}\)
02Define \(A_b\) and \(A_{ef}\)
03Check eccentricity and edges
04Calculate \(\beta\)
05Calculate \(N_{Rdc}\)
06Check \(N_{Edc}\leq N_{Rdc}\)
07Increase bearing area if needed
08Check padstone and wall below
\[N_{Edc}\leq N_{Rdc}\]

7.4 Design Value of the Concentrated Load

Use the design value of the reaction, not the unfactored characteristic reaction. For a simple case with one permanent component and one leading variable component:

\[N_{Edc}=\gamma_G G_{kc}+\gamma_Q Q_{kc}\]

For real projects, the project National Annex and full action combination should be used. Wind, imposed-load reduction and accompanying variable actions may change the governing reaction.

7.5 Bearing Area and Effective Area

The actual loaded area is the contact area directly below the bearing. The effective area is the larger zone available after load spread. The effective length must be limited by available masonry and cannot pass through a wall end, free edge, movement joint or opening.

Actual area

\[A_b=l_c w_c\]

Effective area

\[A_{ef}=l_{ef}t\]

Area ratio

\[A_{ratio}=\min\left(\frac{A_b}{A_{ef}},0.45\right)\]

7.6 Concentrated Load Resistance

The local bearing resistance is calculated using the actual loaded area, design compressive strength and enhancement factor.

Design compressive strength

\[f_d=\frac{fk}{\gamma_M}\]

Local bearing resistance

\[N_{Rdc}=\beta A_b f_d\]
Important: this local bearing check does not replace the normal vertical resistance check of the wall.

7.7 Enhancement Factor \(\beta\)

Beta is a limited local enhancement due to load spread and confinement. It is not a general increase to masonry strength.

\[\beta_{init}=\max\left[\left(1+0.3\frac{a_1}{h_c}\right)\left(1.5-1.1A_{ratio}\right),1.0\right]\]
\[\beta_{max}=\min\left(1.25+\frac{a_1}{2h_c},1.5\right)\]
\[\beta=\min(\beta_{init},\beta_{max})\]

7.8 Conditions Before Using \(\beta\)

Before applying a beneficial \(\beta\), check that the eccentricity, edge distance, available load-spread zone, material category and normal wall resistance are suitable.

Eccentricity limit

\[e_c\leq\frac{t}{4}\]

Slenderness check

\[\frac{h_{ef}}{t_{ef}}\leq27\]

7.9 When a Padstone or Spreader Is Needed

A padstone or spreader is used when the masonry cannot safely resist the load through the original beam bearing area. It increases the loaded area and distributes the reaction into a wider length of wall.

  • Direct bearing fails: \(N_{Edc}>N_{Rdc}\).
  • The bearing length is too small.
  • The load is close to an end, opening or movement joint.
  • The local masonry strength is low or execution category is unfavourable.
\[A_{b,req}=\frac{N_{Edc}}{1.5f_d}\]
Design note: the padstone/spreader itself still needs its own bending, shear, bearing and detailing checks according to its material.

7.11 Common Mistakes to Flag

Using characteristic load

Always check local bearing using the design concentrated load \(N_{Edc}\), not the unfactored reaction.

Ignoring edges/openings

The effective length must stop at openings, free edges and movement joints.

Using \(\beta\) automatically

Check eccentricity, edge distance, slenderness and wall validity first.

Wrong leaf thickness

Use only the loaded leaf if only one leaf carries the bearing load.

Forgetting global wall check

The local check is separate from the normal vertical resistance check.

Incomplete padstone design

Increasing bearing area is not the full padstone design; check the padstone material too.

Reference notes for manual checking

Manual checking should refer to EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.3, §6.1.2, §5.5.1.4, Figure 6.2 and Annex G, together with the project National Annex and product data.
