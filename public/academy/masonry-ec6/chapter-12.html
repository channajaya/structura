12.1 Purpose of this chapter

This chapter explains how cavity walls are treated in masonry design. A cavity wall has two leaves separated by a cavity and connected by wall ties. The leaves may share lateral load, but only if the ties are correctly selected, spaced and detailed.

This chapter is the interface between wind pressure from Chapter 11 and cavity wall panel checks. It also separates two ideas that are often confused: effective thickness for vertical resistance and lateral load sharing between leaves.

12.2 Behaviour of a cavity wall

A cavity wall should not be treated as a single solid wall unless the detailing justifies that assumption. In most practical design, the engineer checks how the two leaves interact through wall ties.

Clean teaching sketch: two masonry leaves linked by ties under lateral wind pressure.

Combined resistance approach

The design lateral resistance is taken as the sum of the design resistance of each leaf, provided the tie action and support assumptions are valid.

Load apportionment approach

The design lateral pressure is shared between leaves according to stiffness or capacity, and each leaf is checked separately.

12.3 Effective thickness for vertical resistance

For vertical load resistance, the effective thickness of a cavity wall is normally based on the two leaf thicknesses:

tef = (t13 + t23)1/3

Here t1 is the thickness of the outer or unloaded leaf, and t2 is the thickness of the inner or loaded leaf. Ties must be provided at adequate density and spacing before this behaviour is assumed.

Do not confuse this vertical-resistance effective thickness with lateral load sharing. The lateral pressure split depends on stiffness, resistance, support condition and tie adequacy.

12.4 Lateral load sharing between leaves

If the leaves have similar material stiffness and are properly tied, a simple stiffness-based load split may be used. Since bending stiffness is approximately proportional to t3, the thicker/stiffer leaf attracts more lateral load.

WEd,1 = WEd t13t13 + t23
WEd,2 = WEd t23t13 + t23

If the leaves have different masonry strengths, different moduli, different support conditions or different damage/exposure conditions, the simple stiffness split should be reviewed carefully.

12.5 Wall ties

Wall ties transfer lateral action between leaves and help the leaves act together. They are not just construction accessories; they are part of the lateral load path.

Ftie,Ed = WEdAtie,    Atie = svsh

Spacing

Closer spacing reduces the tributary area and therefore reduces the design force per tie.

Resistance

The declared design resistance of the selected tie must exceed the design tie force.

Durability and detailing

Tie type and durability class must suit the cavity exposure, movement and local detailing around openings.

12.7 Chapter summary

Key points

  • A cavity wall has two leaves connected by ties.
  • The leaves can share lateral load only if the ties are adequate.
  • Effective thickness is used for vertical resistance and slenderness checks.
  • Lateral load may be apportioned by stiffness or checked using combined resistance.

Common mistakes

Treating a cavity wall as a solid wall without checking tie action.
Using tef for lateral load split without understanding the assumptions.
Checking tie resistance but forgetting minimum tie density.
Ignoring extra detailing near openings, corners and movement joints.
