Slenderness, Effective Height and Buckling Detail

This chapter explains how support restraint changes the effective height of masonry walls, how that changes slenderness, and why the vertical resistance can change even when the wall geometry is unchanged.

8.1 Purpose and learning outcomes

Effective height is not always equal to the clear wall height. It depends on the way the wall is held by floors, roofs, side walls, piers and surrounding masonry.

Restraint condition

Decide whether the wall is restrained at top and bottom only, or also stiffened by one or both vertical edges.

Slenderness effect

Small changes in hef can noticeably change λ, second-order eccentricity and vertical resistance.

Detailing judgement

Openings, chases and weak junctions should not be ignored when claiming restraint.

8.2 Effective height of a wall

Effective height is the design height used for buckling checks. A wall with better restraint has a smaller effective height and therefore a lower slenderness ratio.

hef = ρn h

For walls restrained at top and bottom only, the commonly used factor is ρ2. The value depends mainly on the type of floor or roof restraint and the eccentricity at the top of the wall.

Case A · RC restraint

Use when sufficient concrete floor/roof restraint and small top eccentricity are available.

Case B · timber/pinned

Use conservative full clear height where restraint is weaker or rotation is less controlled.

Case C · high eccentricity

If top eccentricity is too high, the reduced effective height should not be used.

  • RC floor or roof restraint with sufficient bearing and small eccentricity: ρ2 = 0.75.
  • Timber floor or roof restraint: ρ2 = 1.0.
  • If the top eccentricity is large, adopt the conservative case with ρ2 = 1.0.
  • Where vertical stiffening walls are present, ρ3 or ρ4 may be used only if the stiffening-wall requirements are satisfied.

8.3 Effective thickness

Effective thickness is the thickness used in the slenderness calculation. For a solid single-leaf wall, it is normally the actual wall thickness.

tef = t for a simple single-leaf wall

For cavity walls, both leaves can contribute only if they are adequately tied and the assumed load sharing is valid. The unloaded leaf should not be over-counted. For piered or stiffened walls, any enhanced effective thickness should be used only when the detailing conditions are satisfied.

Design warning: do not use the total cavity wall thickness unless the wall type, ties and load path justify composite behaviour.

8.4 Slenderness and second-order effect

The slenderness ratio compares effective height with effective thickness. For walls subjected mainly to vertical loading, the simplified EC6 method is normally limited to:

λ = heftef ≤ 27

Higher slenderness increases sensitivity to eccentricity and buckling. Construction imperfections are also represented through an additional eccentricity term.

ea = hef22000tef

8.5 Openings, chases and edge restraint

Openings and chases can change the structural behaviour of a wall. If an opening or chase interrupts the load path or removes too much effective thickness, the affected line may need to be treated as a free edge.

When restraint may be lost

  • Large opening close to the supported wall.
  • Vertical chase cutting through the restraint zone.
  • Poor or cracked junction between wall and stiffener.
  • Movement joint or free edge beside the wall panel.

What to check

  • Stiffening wall length and thickness.
  • Connection/tie detail at the junction.
  • Opening position and remaining masonry length.
  • Whether recognised analysis is needed for irregular panels.

8.7 Common mistakes to flag

Wrong height assumption

Using the clear height directly when a reduced effective height is justified, or using a reduced height when the restraint condition is not actually present.

Ignoring high eccentricity

Using ρ2 = 0.75 even when the top eccentricity exceeds the permitted limit.

Over-counting thickness

Using total cavity wall thickness when only one leaf is carrying vertical load.

Forgetting openings/chases

Assuming stiffening walls or edges remain effective even when openings or chases break the load path.

Reference notes for manual checking

EN 1996-1-1 provisions on effective height, effective thickness and slenderness; Roberts & Brooker vertical resistance guidance; Morton commentary on support conditions and slenderness.
