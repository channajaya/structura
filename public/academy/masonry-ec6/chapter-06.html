Chapter Position and Purpose

This chapter applies the vertical-load theory from Chapter 5 to three design situations. The aim is to show the complete flow from geometry and material inputs to the final check \(N_{Ed} \leq N_{Rd}\).

The worked examples use the same Reference / Calculation / Output calculation-sheet format used in later chapters. Each clause chip opens the relevant teaching note or reference figure in a pop-up window.

6.1 Worked Example Format

Each example follows the same sequence so that the reader can compare the single-leaf, cavity-wall and column cases without changing the method.

1
Set inputsGeometry, support condition, material strength and design action.
2
Find \(h_{ef}\) and \(t_{ef}\)Use restraint and wall-type rules.
3
Check slendernessUse \(h_{ef}/t_{ef}\), normally limited to 27 for this route.
4
Find eccentricity and \(\Phi\)Check end and mid-height reduction factors.
5
Calculate \(N_{Rd}\)Use design strength and the governing capacity factor.
6
ComparePass if \(N_{Ed} \leq N_{Rd}\).

WE-02

Single-Leaf Load-Bearing Wall

Vertical resistance of a 215 mm single-leaf wall —

Given: wall thickness \(t=215\,\text{mm}\), clear height \(h=2700\,\text{mm}\), restraint factor \(\rho_n=0.75\), design compressive strength \(f_d=2.46\,\text{N/mm}^2\), and design vertical action \(N_{Ed}=180\,\text{kN/m}\). Concentric loading is assumed for this teaching example.

Single-leaf wall calculationper metre run
ReferenceCalculationOutput
\(h_{ef}=\rho_n h=0.75\times2700\)\(h_{ef}=2025\,\text{mm}\)
\(t_{ef}=t\)\(t_{ef}=215\,\text{mm}\)
\(h_{ef}/t_{ef}=2025/215\)\(9.42\leq27\) OK
Concentric loading: \(e_i=0\)\(\Phi_i=1.000\)
\(e_a=h_{ef}^2/(2000t_{ef})=2025^2/(2000\times215)\)\(e_a=9.54\,\text{mm}\)
Annex G\(e_{mk}=e_i+e_a=0+9.54\)\(e_{mk}=9.54\,\text{mm}\)
Annex G equation route\(\Phi_m=1.141-2e_{mk}/t-0.024(h_{ef}/t_{ef})\)—
Annex G\(\Phi_m=1.141-2(9.54)/215-0.024(9.42)\)\(\Phi_m=0.813\)
\(\Phi=\min(\Phi_i,\Phi_m)=\min(1.000,0.813)\)\(\Phi=0.813\)
EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.2\(N_{Rd}=\Phi t f_d=0.813\times215\times2.46\)\(N_{Rd}=429\,\text{kN/m}\)
EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.2.1\(N_{Ed}=180\leq N_{Rd}=429\)PASS; utilisation = 0.42
Result: the single-leaf wall is adequate for the assumed design vertical action.

Professor's note: the cavity wall has a lower capacity reduction factor than the single-leaf wall because its effective thickness is smaller relative to the wall height.
WE-04

Isolated Masonry Column with Small-Section Factor

Vertical resistance of a 215 mm × 215 mm masonry column — and

Given: isolated column \(215\,\text{mm}\times215\,\text{mm}\), clear height \(h=2700\,\text{mm}\), pin-ended condition \(\rho_n=1.0\), loading eccentricity \(e_i=25\,\text{mm}\), base characteristic strength \(fk=6.63\,\text{N/mm}^2\), \(\gamma_M=2.7\), and design axial load \(N_{Ed}=30\,\text{kN}\).

Annex G\(e_{mk}=25+16.95\)\(e_{mk}=41.95\,\text{mm}\)
Annex G\(\Phi_m=1.141-2(41.95)/215-0.024(12.56)\)\(\Phi_m=0.394\)

EN 1996-1-1 §6.1.2\(N_{Rd}=\Phi A f_d/1000\)kN conversion

Result: the column is adequate, but the utilisation is high. The small-section factor significantly reduces the usable material strength.

6.6 Common Mistakes to Flag

×Skipping the slenderness check before calculating resistance.
×Using actual thickness \(t\) instead of effective thickness \(t_{ef}\) in the slenderness check.
×Forgetting the mid-height reduction factor \(\Phi_m\).
×Missing the small-section factor for columns and narrow piers where \(A<0.1\,\text{m}^2\).
Reference notes for manual checking: EN 1996-1-1 §3.6.1.2 small-section factor, §5.5.1.2 effective height, §5.5.1.3 effective thickness, §5.5.2.1 slenderness, §6.1.2 vertical resistance and Annex G reduction factors. The vertical wall design sequence follows EN 1996-1-1 §6.1. Clause text is summarised for teaching; check the licensed standard and National Annex before real design.
